# Two operational risks: the trade-state database's write lock, and alerts posted while the listener is down (2026-11-22)

Owner, 2026-11-22: "Diagnostic logging being nonblocking does not establish that the trading path is safe" and
"Deploying outside market hours reduces exposure but does not solve crashes or unexpected restarts." Both risks were
named as open in `closing_questions_2026-11-22.md`. This document measures the first, fixes what was demonstrated
(its own change), and gives a bounded recovery design for the second (a design: not built).

## 1. The trade-state database

### How the trading path uses it
The live process runs its loops in threads: short book every 10 s, long book every 15 s, official-tape stop check
every 30 s (`scripts/fade_live.py`). Each pass reads the open trades and writes any state change through
`OpsStore.tx()`. Every `tx()` takes the database's single write lock at once (BEGIN IMMEDIATE), **also when the
block only reads**, and waits up to 30 s for it. So one slow block anywhere delays every loop behind it: the read of
the open trades (stop EVALUATION), the write of the exit, and the exit text that follows the write. After 30 s the
waiting pass raises; the loop logs it and tries again at its next tick. No state is lost (it is all in the database),
but nothing protective happens while the lock is held.

Who else takes the lock during market hours (`deploy/fly/crontab`, ET): the site's pages on every load, `sms` every
minute, `charts` and `lab` every 10 minutes, `borrow` every 15, `pilotbook` every 5, `rebalance` hourly.

### Demonstration (local, not on the runner)
A short is open and the price is through its stop. Another connection holds the write lock.

| Lock held by another writer | Stop pass took | Exit text sent after |
|---|---|---|
| 0 s | 0.03 s | 0.03 s |
| 3 s | 3.06 s | 3.06 s |

The delay is one for one. This is a property of the design, shown; whether it happens in practice is the measurement.

### Measurement on the runner (2026-11-22, 17:10 to 17:15 ET, a Sunday: market closed, nothing open)
A probe asked for the write lock every 20 ms for 260 s (12,481 times) and released it at once. During the window
the `charts` and `lab` jobs were run as they run in the session, the every-minute `sms` job ran on its own schedule,
and the site's two main reads were called.

| Wait for the write lock | |
|---|---|
| median | 0.47 ms |
| 99th percentile | 0.92 ms |
| 99.9th percentile | 6.7 ms |
| maximum | **330 ms** |
| over 1 s | 0 |
| errors | 0 |

| What ran | Longest wait it caused |
|---|---|
| `charts` job (9 s long) | 21 ms |
| `lab` job (6 s long) | 22 ms |
| site: the notes / journal page (`journal.entries`) | **330 ms**: the page held the lock 0.29 s while it read |
| site: the overview page (`overview.build`) | under 20 ms |

A static pass over all 174 transaction blocks found no network call inside one. The holder that was found is a READ:
the journal page reads every trade and estimates a cost per row inside the block. Its hold grows with every trade
(0.29 s at 53 settled trades; the query allows 500 per table).

**What this does and does not establish.**
- Established: the mechanism is real, and one page read held the lock for a third of a second.
- NOT established: the distribution during a session. The measurement was taken on a quiet Sunday. The live loops,
  the alert handler, the `borrow` job and approvals by text were idle or not exercised. No 30-second wait was seen;
  that is not evidence one cannot happen.
- Not measured at all until now, on any day: nothing recorded these waits.

### What is fixed, separately (`fix/db-read-lock`, its own pull request)
1. `OpsStore.read()`: a read-only block that takes no write lock (WAL mode: a reader and the writer do not block each
   other; writes inside it are refused). The journal page uses it. Regression check: with that page stopped in the
   middle of its build, the short book's stop pass books the exit and sends the text in under a second.
2. Every wait for, or hold of, the write lock of 0.25 s or more is written down with who (`db_waits.jsonl`, next to
   the database; bounded; the recording cannot break the write it measures), and the nightly summary's health line
   says `database: no write waited 0.25 s` or names the longest wait and the longest hold. A wait of 1 s or more is a
   warning. This is the session measurement the Sunday probe could not be.

### What stays open, tracked
| Item | Status | Stops being open when |
|---|---|---|
| Session-hours distribution of lock waits | UNMEASURED until the change above runs | 5 sessions of the nightly `database:` clause |
| Other blocks that only read but still use `tx()` (about 170 blocks in all; how many only read was not counted) | left as they are; only the demonstrated holder was changed | the nightly clause names one: it is moved to `read()` with its own test |
| A writer that holds the lock for 30 s | not seen; would stall every loop until it lets go | covered by the same clause (it records the hold and who) |
| Recovery after a crash while the lock is held | SQLite releases a dead process's lock; the state is in the database and the loops resume from it. Start-up itself takes the lock (schema check) and would wait behind a holder | no action; noted |
| A stop resting at a broker | does not exist (paper). The only real protection against any of this | deployment blocker, unchanged |

## 2. Alerts posted while the listener is down

### What happens today
- The alert's identity exists: Telegram gives every post of the channel a message id that only grows, and the book
  stores `channel` and `message_id` on each row.
- What is "already handled" lives in memory only (`chan_poll.State.seen`, `last_id`). On start the 15-second check
  "only sets the baseline (nothing older than the start is replayed)". So a post made while the process is down for
  a deploy, a crash or a machine restart is never handled: no row, no trade, no line. VRAX on 2026-11-04 is the one
  case in 801 alerts.
- From the change merged in #197 the nightly summary names such an alert the same night. That detects; it does not
  recover.

### Design (bounded; NOT built)
**Identifier.** One alert = (`channel`, `message_id`, `symbol`): a post can carry more than one stock. A unique index
on those three columns of `fade_trades` (existing rows keep theirs; rows without a message id are exempt) makes a
second handling of the same alert impossible at the database, whatever the code above it does.

**Durable cursor.** `alert_cursor:<channel>` in the flags table = the highest message id fully handled. It moves in
the SAME transaction as the alert's row (or its "duplicate" / "not a tested kind" outcome), so a crash between the
two cannot happen: either both are stored or neither.

**Replay on start.** Before the live paths are attached, ask the channel for posts with an id above the cursor,
oldest first. Bounds: at most 200 posts, none older than the start of the current session day; one request, with the
existing "slow down" handling. More than 200 or an unreachable channel: stop, raise the gap in the health line, do
not guess. An unset cursor (first start after the change) = today's behaviour: baseline only.

**Deduplication, three layers.** The cursor (nothing at or under it is fetched); the in-memory `seen` set, filled by
the replay before the push and poll paths start (a post that arrives by push during the replay is handled once);
the unique index (last line of defence; a violation is recorded as a duplicate, never raised into the listener).

**Expiration: a recovered alert that is stale is recorded, never traded.**

| Age of the alert when it is recovered | Short book | Long book |
|---|---|---|
| up to 5 minutes (the tested entry window) | handled as live: the entry is the first print AFTER the bot's decision, at that moment's price, exactly as for any alert that arrives 30 s late | watched as any alert |
| over 5 minutes | row `filtered`, reason "recovered after a restart, too old to trade" | not a long event (the reason is not among those the long book qualifies) |

No path books a trade at a past price: the short's entry rule already refuses an entry more than 5 minutes after the
post. The tests of this change must assert that a recovered alert of 6 minutes produces a row and no entry, and
that one of 2 minutes enters at the print after recovery, not at the alert's time.

**Record.** Every recovered alert leaves a decision line with `recovered=true` and its age. The nightly completeness
check (already live with #197's deploy) then reads "complete" where it would have named the alert.

**Effect on the forward measurement.** None on its population: forward events are built from the channel's record,
where a missed alert is already an event "without receipt". With recovery it becomes an event received late, with a
row. The registered samples count priced trades from the bot's real decision time, so a late-recovered alert is
priced late or not traded; nothing is back-filled.

**What it does not cover.** A post Telegram never delivers to this account; an outage longer than the bounds (it is
reported, not recovered); the crowd recorder's own gaps (it has its hourly catch-up).

**Until it is built.** Deploys only outside 09:25 to 16:00 ET (reduces deploy restarts to none in the session;
does nothing for a crash), and the nightly line names any alert that slips. One more unrecorded alert, or the owner's
word, and the design is built as its own change with the tests above.
