# Plan: from one proven signal to a learning trading platform (2026-11-04)

## Goal (the owner's words, made measurable)
"Make money from live data, learn and get smarter with every trade, work on tickers we have not seen, as close
to always-right as possible." Measurable version: maximize net profit per unit of risk on trades the system
has never seen, with losses capped, proven forward. Win rate is not the target: a high win rate is easy to buy
with tiny wins and rare huge losses, which is how strategies blow up.

## Where the evidence stands
- About 20 pre-registered tests. One fast edge survived held-out data: fading the MetaTradings alerts
  (+1.12%/trade net, lower bound +0.05%, n = 764, 2026-07..10), but only on hard-to-borrow names.
- Per-ticker "brains" on large caps: real but tiny skill, below costs, at 30 min, 1-2 h, to the close and
  around earnings (3 independent fails). Not pursued further.
- Slow: 60/40, turn-of-month (small), megacap daily (near-miss, forward test to 2027-04/10).

## What the research says (2026-11-04)
1. The fade has a known cause: retail attention buying sprees in small caps (< $1B) are followed by negative
   returns (Barber, Huang, Odean, Schwarz, "Attention Induced Trading and Returns", Robinhood data:
   -3% over 5 days for the most-bought stocks, absent above $1B). The alerts mark exactly those sprees.
2. The catch: hard-to-borrow stocks fall partly AS COMPENSATION for the borrow fee (Engelberg, Reed and
   Ringgenberg; Muravyev et al.), and squeezes cluster there. So the real borrow availability and cost decide
   whether the fade pays, and we have never measured them.
3. IBKR publishes availability and borrow fee for ~20,000 US stocks on a documented public FTP
   (ftp2.interactivebrokers.com, user `shortstock`, `usa.txt`), refreshed through the day, no account needed.
   IBKR charges borrow on SETTLED shorts, so a same-day 60-minute short likely pays no borrow fee, only
   needs available shares [VERIFY on a real statement]. Our test charged 0.5%/trade for it.
4. Meta-labeling (a second model that learns WHICH signals of a primary rule to take, and how big) helps most
   when the primary signal comes from outside market data, e.g. discretionary alerts. That is our case.
5. Under concept drift, a recent-window retrain beats retraining on all history; delayed labels matter.
   Champion/challenger with a pre-registered promotion rule is the standard control.
6. Repeated looks at forward data inflate false positives; any "promote" decision needs a fixed sample size
   or an always-valid sequential test (Johari, Pekelis, Walsh).

## Plan (ordered by value; each step has a pass/fail gate)
**W1 Reality layer for the fade (now, $0).** Snapshot IBKR availability and fee every 15 minutes in market
hours on the runner; stamp every live fade trade with IBKR shortable / shares available / fee and Alpaca's
easy-to-borrow flag at alert time. The book then reports the "actually tradeable at IBKR" subset separately.
This answers "is the edge real AND tradeable" with live data. The borrow history is also saved, and becomes a
feature.

**W2 The decisive forward record on the server, not the laptop (now).** The listener stores every raw channel
message (and edits) on the volume; the forward research record (amendment F1) is rebuilt nightly on Fly from
that, so it never misses a day because the Mac is asleep.

**W3 The "brain": one pooled learned filter across all tickers (pre-registered).** Rather than a model per ticker
(too little data per name, so it fails), one model learns from every alert which fades to take and how
big, using ticker traits known at alert time (price, prior run-up, gap, relative volume, time of day, alert
type, halts that day, borrow fee/availability, market tone). Because it learns traits, not names, it applies
to tickers it has never seen. Development: training alerts 2024-01..2026-06 with purged time-series CV; the
2026-07..10 held-out is reported only (it was already seen once). Decisive test: forward, as a shadow
challenger.

**W4 Learning platform: champion/challenger in shadow (the "gets smarter" part).** Every live alert is scored
by the champion (the frozen rule) and by challengers (W3 filter, exit variants, window vs expanding retrain).
Each gets its own virtual book. Monthly retrain of challengers on all data so far; promotion only by a
pre-registered rule (fixed n, then challenger beats champion with the bootstrap CI above zero, reusing
`sgt.learning.promotion`); every swap logged in the trial ledger. Decay monitors (`sgt.learning.decay`) on the
champion can demote it.

**W5 Execution realism.** Measure live estimate vs official SIP result per trade (slippage), approval latency
(alert -> OK -> entry) and its cost; feed both into the cost model.
Built 2026-11-04 (`src/sgt/fast/realism.py`, nightly digest line, `fade_live.py realism`): per settled trade the
texted estimate minus the official gross, alert -> entry time by mode (auto / approved / armed), and the waiting
cost = official net at the real entry minus official net at the tested timing (`rule_net`). Also fixed: live
time-exits now stop at the regular-session close, as the research window does (a 15:30 entry exits at 16:00, not
16:30). Reported at n >= 50 settled trades; any cost-model change is pre-registered first.

**Deferred, with reason.** Real orders (needs a broker with borrow; owner declined for now), halt feed (paid
data), more large-cap intraday models (three fails).

## Decision points
- Fade forward n >= 300 (about 6-8 weeks): KEEP / DROP per amendment F1, now reported for the IBKR-tradeable
  subset too.
- W3 filter: promoted only if it beats the champion forward by the pre-registered rule.
- Megacap daily: 2027-04-30 and 2027-10-29.
